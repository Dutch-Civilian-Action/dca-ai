# Audiences and relationship stage

Load this when preparing a message for a specific audience, or more than one version of the
same message.

## Establish the stage before the tone

Audience and relationship stage are two separate facts, and both come from evidence:

| Stage | What the evidence must show | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First contact | No prior DCA outreach, reply or support on record | Introduce DCA and the need; no assumed familiarity; no "again" or "as always" |
| Existing relationship | Recorded prior contact, support, visit or agreement | Reference what actually happened, by its own record; continue the existing thread where one exists |
| Reply | An inbound message being answered | Answer what was asked first; keep their framing and language |
| Follow-up | An earlier ask, promise or agreed next step on record | Name the specific open item, not a general reminder |

If the stage cannot be established from relationship history or the supplied thread, say so
and draft for the weaker assumption — first contact — rather than implying a relationship
that is not evidenced.

## What adapts and what does not

**May adapt:** opening, emphasis, ordering, length, examples chosen from the evidenced set,
call to action, level of background, language.

**Must not change between versions:** the need, the figures, the dates, the images and what
they depict, what DCA is asking for, what has already been committed or delivered, and any
condition attached to a donation.

If two audiences would need different facts to be true, the difference is a factual problem,
not an adaptation.

## Churches

Use supported community or diaconal context: what the congregation or its community
organisations have actually done or supported, where that is on record.

Do not assume religious affiliation, denomination, theology, a preaching slot, a collection
schedule, or that a prior gift from one congregation implies another.

## Rotary clubs

Use supported club, presentation or partnership context: a presentation actually given or
scheduled, a club project actually recorded, a member contact actually established.

Do not assume club priorities, a speaking invitation, membership of a named person, or that
one club's support indicates another's.

## Donors, funders and partners

- **Donors** — reference only recorded giving. Do not restate an amount, a date or a
  frequency that the relationship record does not support, and do not thank someone for
  something not recorded.
- **Funders** — work to their stated criteria, scope and deadline where those are supplied;
  where they are not, mark them as unestablished rather than inferring them from a
  comparable funder.
- **Partners** — distinguish an agreed partnership from a discussion. A conversation, a
  shared event or a mutual contact is not a partnership.

## Presentation preparation

The same rules hold for presentation material: one factual core, adapted framing, no figure
or image that its source does not support, and a single primary ask per audience.

## Personal data

Keep recipient lists, addresses, personal circumstances and consent evidence out of the
recipient-facing draft and out of any shared summary that a broader audience can read.

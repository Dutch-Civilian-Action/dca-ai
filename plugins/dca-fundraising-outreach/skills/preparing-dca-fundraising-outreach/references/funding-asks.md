# Funding asks, applications and follow-through

Load this when the message contains a funding request, a grant or fund application, or when
outreach has produced a reply, a meeting or a presentation that needs following up.

## The five states, never collapsed

| State | Meaning | Evidence it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proposed support | DCA has asked, or intends to ask | The ask itself |
| Donor commitment | A funder or donor has said yes | Their own statement, on record |
| Received donation | Money has arrived | A financial record, not a pledge |
| Allocation | Money is assigned to a purpose | An allocation decision |
| Actual expenditure | Money has been spent | A finance record |

Never present one as another, and never sum across them. "Raised" and "spent" are not
interchangeable; a pledge is not income; an allocation is not a purchase.

Finance validates amounts paid and remaining funding gaps. Operational owners validate needs,
delivery and mission claims. Fundraising owns the ask and the relationship.

## Structuring a request

Keep these five apart, in this order, and name the source of each:

1. the underlying need — what is required, for whom, and why the gap remains;
2. the request to this funder specifically;
3. what is already covered, and by whom;
4. the remaining gap;
5. the evidence this funder needs in order to decide.

Do not reuse an amount from an earlier ask, an old proposal or a draft budget as the current
figure. If the current figure is not established, say so and mark it rather than carrying the
old one forward.

Do not imply that a donation is unrestricted, earmarked, matched or tax-deductible unless
applicable confirmation exists.

## Applications

For a fund or grant application, work to the funder's stated criteria, scope, format and
deadline where those are supplied. Where they are not supplied, mark them unestablished
rather than inferring them from another funder's programme.

Answer only what the evidence supports. An unanswerable section is an explicit gap with a
named person who could resolve it — not a plausible paragraph.

## After a reply, a meeting or a presentation

Identify and record:

- the outcome, in the recipient's own terms where they stated one;
- any outstanding commitment, on either side;
- the next action;
- its owner, only where actually established;
- the agreed timing, only where actually agreed.

Preserve these through the existing authorised record route — the Relationship Data
capability for contact, organisation and relationship records, under its identity, provenance
and confirmation rules. There is no separate handoff step for follow-through that route
already permits, and no parallel record store.

Where the record cannot be written — no access, or no authorization — give a concise proposed
record with source links, say plainly that it is not recorded, and say which of the two is the
limit.

Do not invent a deadline, and do not mark work complete from intent alone. A drafted reply is
not a sent reply; a scheduled meeting is not a decision.

## Thank-you messages

Thank only for what is recorded, in the amount and terms recorded. An unrecorded or
approximate gift is a reason to check, not to round. Do not attach an impact claim to a
donation unless the delivery it refers to is evidenced.

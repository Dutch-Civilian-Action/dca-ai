# Campaign selection and the Mailchimp boundary

Load this when outreach is built on a newsletter campaign, or when a campaign, its images or
its links are referenced.

## Selecting the campaign

1. Retrieve campaigns from Mailchimp directly where access exists — content, images, links,
   status and dates. Do not ask for a campaign to be uploaded or pasted when it can be
   retrieved.
2. Identify each candidate by **name, send status and date**. Status and date, not title
   similarity, decide which is the latest.
3. Take the **latest sent** campaign as the basis for outreach.
4. **Name any newer draft explicitly.** A newer draft is neither silently used nor silently
   ignored: say it exists, give its name and date, and say it is a draft.
5. **Confirm before drafting only when relevance is genuinely ambiguous** — several campaigns
   could plausibly be meant, or the reference ("the recent newsletter") does not resolve
   against what is there. When the evidence resolves it, proceed and state which campaign was
   used. Do not manufacture a confirmation question for a resolved case.
6. If Mailchimp access is unavailable, name it as a configuration gap, state that campaign
   selection could not be performed, and continue from supplied material only.

## What a sent campaign does and does not establish

A sent campaign establishes that it went out, with that content, on that date.

It does not establish that:

- its claims are still true today;
- its figures are current;
- the work it describes has since been delivered;
- it is approved for a different audience.

Recheck every time-sensitive claim — amounts, gaps, quantities, delivery status, dates,
locations — against the relevant current operational or financial source before repeating it.
Where that source is unreachable, mark the claim as unrechecked rather than repeating it as
current.

## Images and links

Carry images and links over as they appear in the campaign. Verify what an image actually
depicts before describing it. Never invent a caption, a date, a location, a name or an
outcome for an image, and never move an image from the context it was published in to one
the evidence does not support.

## Retrieval only

Mailchimp is a **read** source for this capability. Do not create, edit, duplicate,
schedule, send or otherwise change a campaign, and do not alter an audience, a list, a
segment or a contact.

The connector's tool surface includes campaign-editing and save operations. Their presence is
capability, not authorization. If a change is requested, decline it, say whether the limit is
access or authorization, and do not attempt it to find out.

Adding or updating subscribers is the Relationship Data capability's newsletter-intake route,
under its own consent and provenance rules. It never runs from here.

## Other sources

- **Gmail** — recipient and thread history, and drafting context.
- **Google Drive** — approved communication material, brand material, fundraising working
  material.
- **Airtable** — relationship and outreach history, through the Relationship Data route only.
- **Calendar** — only where scheduling actually matters to the message.

Every connector runs under the account of the person using it. Credentials and capabilities
do not transfer between users or between surfaces: what one person's session can reach says
nothing about another's.
